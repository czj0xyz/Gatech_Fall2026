\subsection*{Exercise 1.3.13-1}
Suppose there are two different minimal polynomials $p_1,p_2$ of $T$.
We use $\deg f$ to denote the degree of polynomial $f$.
By minimality, $\deg p_1 = \deg p_2$.
Since both of $p_1$ and $p_2$ are monic, we have that $\deg (p_1-p_2) < \deg p_1$ and $(p_1-p_2)(T) = p_1(T)-p_2(T) = 0$.
Since $p_1-p_2\neq 0$, it contradicts to the minimality of $p_1$.
Therefore, the minimal polynomial is unique.

By Block Diagonal Form (Corollary 1.5.45), there exists $V$ such that $V^{-1}AV = R = \diag\{R_{ii}\}_{i\in [p]}$, where $R_{ii} = \lambda_i I + U_{ii}$ and $U_{ii}\in \C^{m_i\times m_i}$ is strictly upper triangle.
Fix $k\in [p]$, let $U_{kk} = (u_{ij})_{i,j\in [m_k]}$ and $v_1\ldots v_{m_k}$ be the $(1+\sum_{i<k}m_i)$-th to $(\sum_{i\leq k}m_i)$-th column vectors of $V$.
Since $AV = VR$, we have that
$$
    \begin{cases}
        Av_1 = \lambda_k v_1
        \\Av_2 = u_{12}v_1 + \lambda_k v_2
        \\\ \ \ \ \ \vdots
        \\Av_{m_k} = \lambda_k v_{m_k} + \sum_{i<m_k} u_{i m_k}v_i
    \end{cases}

    \begin{cases}
        (A - \lambda_k I)v_1 = 0

        \\\ \ \ \ \ \vdots
        \\ (A - \lambda_k I)^{m_k}v_{m_k} = (A - \lambda_k I)^{m_k-1}\sum_{i<m_k} u_{i m_k}v_i = 0
    \end{cases}
$$
Therefore, $v_1,\cdots,v_{m_k}$ are solutions of $(A-\lambda_k I)^{m_k}v = 0$ and the solution set is spannde by at least $m_k$ linearly independent vectors.

$$
    (A-\lambda_k I)^{m_k}V=V(R - \lambda_k I)^{m_k} \implies

    (A-\lambda_k I)^{m_k}v = (\lambda - \lambda_k)^{m_k}v + \text{linear combination of column vectors of }V \neq 0,
$$ 
since $\lambda - \lambda_k\neq 0$ and linear independence of column vectors of $V$.
Since $\dim(V)=n$, the dimension of solution set of $(A-\lambda_k I)^{m_k}v = 0$ is $m_k$.

We prove by induction on $k$. 
The statement follows from definition of Jordan block if $k=1$.

Now suppose $k > 1$.
We have that 
\begin{align}
    (J^k)_{ij} &= \sum_{\ell=1}^n  (J^{k-1})_{i\ell} (J)_{\ell j}
    =  \sum_{\ell=i}^{i+k-1} \binom{k-1}{\ell - i}\lambda^{k-1-(\ell-i)} (J)_{\ell j} &&(\text{I.H.}) \nonumber
    \\&= \lambda^{k+i-j} \sum_{\ell=i}^{i+k-1} \ind[j-1\leq \ell \leq j]\binom{k-1}{\ell - i} &&(\text{def. of }J) \nonumber
    \\&= 
    \begin{cases}
        \binom{k-1}{j-i-1} + \binom{k-1}{j-i}&,i\leq j-1 \land j\leq i+k-1
        \\\binom{k-1}{j-i}=1=\binom{k}{j-i}&, j=i
        \\\binom{k-1}{j-i-1}=1=\binom{k}{j-i}&, j=i+k
        \\0 &, \text{o.w.}
    \end{cases} . \label{eq:P5_final}
\end{align}
Since $\binom{k-1}{j-i-1} + \binom{k-1}{j-i} = \binom{k}{j-i}$, \Cref{eq:P5_final} completes the proof.
